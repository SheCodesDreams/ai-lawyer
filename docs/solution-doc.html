1. Problem choice and scope

I picked Problem 2 because everyday legal problems in India (money not returned, a scam, a notice nobody explains) hurt many people and need no show-specific context. I went deep on money disputes and said what is not covered. Covered, with verified law: online refunds and non-delivery, e-commerce platforms, payment scams and unauthorised transactions, rental deposits, unpaid salary, private loans where documents are held, and demand notices and cheque bounce. Not covered: family, property title, tax, criminal defence. For those the tool still gives practical steps but labels them "(General)" and does not cite law. It never stops at "see a lawyer".

2. Assumptions (the brief was silent)

  • Users are ordinary people on a phone, often not writing English; text or voice, replies in their language (script detected in code).
  • "Today" is the server date; criminal law means the BNS 2023, not the IPC. Consumer forum limits use the amount paid (District up to Rs 50 lakh).
  • indiacode.nic.in blocked my fetches, so 33 of 53 corpus entries come from secondary sources (bare-act sites, law-firm notes); 20 are official text. Each entry carries source_type and verified_on.
  • Free tiers only: Gemini free API, Vercel Hobby. No user data is stored; the case lives in the browser and is sent with each request.

3. How it works: what AI does, what code does

Flow: (1) tell your story, (2) it restates a structured case file for you to confirm, (3) it explains where you stand, cites laws and asks what proof you have, (4) it gives a step-by-step plan, the right forum, a draft notice/complaint/reply, and a verdict on whether this tool can handle the case, (5) follow-up questions, plus Practice mode: the AI plays the other side's lawyer, briefs you on likely tricks, asks hard questions and scores each answer.

Gemini (3.5 Flash-Lite)Turns the messy story into facts and a timeline; explains in plain language; picks relevant provisions from the file; drafts documents; plays the opposing lawyer.
Plain codeStage order; the law file; citation validation; flagging of unknown section numbers, "IPC" and case names; age of the claim from the event date; language detection and a retry if an English user gets Hindi; verdict rules (amount over Rs 50 lakh, claim near its time limit, threats, a court case already filed, no matching law).
Human in the loopThe corpus is hand-reviewed with sources. The verdict recommends a lawyer, in addition to steps, for the risky cases above. Drafts have [PLACEHOLDERS] and a "check every detail" note.

Swappable model: one llm() function; set LLM_PROVIDER=openai with a key, model and base URL to use any OpenAI-compatible API, or change GEMINI_MODEL.

4. How it avoids invented laws

  • Closed corpus. 53 entries (about 7k tokens) go in every prompt. The model may cite only by entry id; code drops any id not in the file and tells the user.
  • Safety net on free text. Section/rule/article numbers that appear nowhere in the file, "IPC", and "X v. Y" case citations are flagged to the user as unverified.
  • Honest caveats. Each entry says what is state-specific, a model law or unconfirmed (e.g. the Model Tenancy Act applies only where a state adopted it; RBI liability rules change for transactions from 1 Jan 2027; 3 entries are marked unverified). Prompts forbid fixed refund deadlines, case law and certainty.
  • Limit: the model can still paraphrase an entry loosely. The citation check proves the entry exists, not that the summary fits.

5. How I checked quality (real runs, honest failures)

I wrote 17 test conversations and ran them on the live site. Checks: verdict, issue type, a cited law id, reply language, forbidden text. Cases: refunds in English, Hindi and Telugu; three scam types; a Rs 60 lakh claim; a 30-month-old claim; salary; deposit; Telugu private-loan documents; cheque notice; blackmail; a trap asking for "IPC 420 and a Supreme Court case"; a vague one-line message. 6. AI tools used, and where they got it wrong

Claude Code wrote the code and helped draft the corpus; three Claude research sub-agents checked each legal claim against web sources (they were told to write NOT VERIFIED, not guess); Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite runs inside the app. Where they were wrong: (1) Claude's recalled section numbers were wrong twice and the source check caught both: Sale of Goods Act s.56 is damages for non-acceptance (non-delivery is s.57), and Consumer Protection Act s.51 is an appeal, not State jurisdiction. This is why the corpus is built from sources, not memory. (2) Gemini mis-computed the age of a claim (above). (3) The default model gemini-2.5-flash had been retired for new users; larger Gemini models took 15-60 s per call, so I chose a lite one.

7. Scale and cost

A case takes about 5-7 model calls: intake 1-3 (small prompt), confirm + analysis, plan, then follow-ups. The corpus (about 7k tokens) is sent with analysis, plan, follow-up and practice calls, so roughly 25k input and 3k output tokens per case. I have not measured rupee cost; it is those tokens times the chosen model's price. The server is stateless, so it scales horizontally and stores no personal data. At volume: a paid model (one env change); cache the fixed corpus prefix; once the corpus passes a few hundred entries, send only entries matching the detected issue type; queue and rate-limit calls; log anonymised failures for lawyer review; re-verify entries on a schedule (each has verified_on) because law moves (RBI rules 2027, new labour codes, state rent laws).
